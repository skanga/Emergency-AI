# Energy, light and electricity off the grid

## Energy priorities

1. **Light** (flashlights, lamps).
2. **Communication** (radio, charging phones to use offline maps and files).
3. **Preserving food and medicines** (a refrigerator for insulin, for example).
4. **Water pumping**, tools.

Save energy: LED lighting uses up to 10 times less than an incandescent bulb.

## Light without electricity

- **LED flashlights powered by batteries, a dynamo (hand crank) or solar.** Prefer models that use common batteries (AA/AAA) and have rechargeable NiMH batteries.
- **Vegetable oil lamp:** a glass jar with 2–3 cm of cooking oil (or melted lard), a cotton wick (string or a strip of T-shirt) held by a wire or a perforated metal lid, with only 2–3 mm of wick above the oil. Vegetable oil doesn't explode and burns for hours.
- **Butter/lard lamp:** a wick pushed into a block of fat.
- **Candles:** store plenty; never leave them burning unattended.
- **Bottle light (Liter of Light):** a PET bottle with water and a little bleach fitted into the roof lights up rooms during the day like a ~50 W bulb.

## Simple solar system (the most useful in the long term)

Components:
1. **Solar panel** (e.g. 100–200 W, 12 V or 18 V nominal).
2. **Charge controller** (PWM or MPPT) — mandatory, protects the battery.
3. **Battery** (12 V): LiFePO4 lithium (lasts longer, 2,000+ cycles) or stationary/automotive lead-acid (cheaper, do not discharge below 50%).
4. **Inverter** (12 V → 127/220 V, or 120/230 V depending on the country) only if you need plug-in appliances; for USB and 12 V LEDs, connect directly.

**Quick math:** energy (Wh) = power (W) × hours.
- A 100 W panel generates, on average, **300–500 Wh per day** in Brazil (3–5 equivalent peak sun hours).
- Phone: ~15 Wh per charge. 9 W LED bulb for 5 h: 45 Wh. Radio: 5 Wh. Laptop: 50–80 Wh per charge.
- Small efficient refrigerator: ~600–1,000 Wh/day (needs 2–3 panels and a large battery).

Wiring: panel → controller → battery → loads. Use a fuse near the battery. Proper wire gauge (12 V requires thick wires so they don't overheat).

## Car batteries and generators

- A car battery can power 12 V bulbs, a radio and car USB chargers. Recharge it by running the engine (uses fuel).
- **Combustion generator:** always **outdoors**, more than 6 m from doors and windows. Carbon monoxide kills. Store fuel in proper cans; gasoline goes bad in 3–6 months without stabilizer (diesel lasts longer).
- Never plug the generator into a household outlet without a transfer switch: the power flows back into the grid and can kill line workers.

## Mechanical and other energy

- **Bicycle with a dynamo/generator** charges small batteries (considerable effort for little energy).
- **Micro-hydro generator** on a stream with a drop: generates power 24 h a day — excellent if the source exists.
- **Firewood:** the simplest energy for cooking and heating. Manage it: cut branches instead of whole trees, plant fast-growing trees, season the wood for months (green wood yields half and makes a lot of smoke).
- **Solar cooker** for cooking without firewood (see [Food](04-food.md)).

## Devices and data

- Keep this kit (Emergency AI + Kiwix databases) on a **USB flash drive and an external hard drive**, and a copy on a low-power laptop.
- A laptop running the local AI uses ~30–60 W while answering; a small computer (mini PC, Raspberry Pi 5) uses ~5–15 W.
- Protect electronics from moisture (silica gel, airtight bags) and from power surges.
