## snake_transport#1 — answered

A snake bit my leg. My friend can call for transport. Should I walk a mile to the road or have people carry me?

### Displayed answer

**Local passages supplied.** They may not support every part of this answer; citations are not independently verified.

**Do not walk.** Have helpers carry you on a stretcher or support you while sitting still.

Walking accelerates venom effects and worsens outcomes. [1]

**Immediate Steps:**
1.  **Get away from the snake** to avoid further bites, but do not try to catch it. [3]
2.  **Keep the bitten limb and your whole body still.** Do not walk. [1][2][3]
3.  **Remove tight items** (rings, bracelets, shoes) from the bitten area before swelling starts. [1][3]
4.  **Have helpers carry you** to the road or a health facility immediately. Walking is dangerous; transport should be passive. [1]
5.  **Arrange urgent transport** to a hospital where antivenom is available; this is the only effective treatment. [1][3]

**Warnings:**
*   Do not use a tight tourniquet, cut the wound, or suck the venom. [2]
*   Do not apply traditional remedies (herbs, alcohol, coffee grounds) to the bite. [3]
*   Monitor breathing while waiting for transport. [1]

**Seek professional help immediately.** This is a medical emergency requiring antivenom.

> ⚠️ First-aid guidance only; it does not replace medical care. If help is available, seek it.
